The state of published CVEs · 26 October 2026 · All dates UTC unless noted

Vulnerability volume has doubled in a year. Almost none of it is being exploited — yet.

395,286 CVEs are published. Monthly output rose from about 4,000 to 13,606 in October, while only 1,756 carry confirmed-exploitation evidence and 95.6% of scored CVEs sit under a 10% exploit forecast. More than half the corpus — 215,998 records — has no vendor-supplied severity at all.

01Publication volume · 24 months

Publication volume, November 2024 to October 2026

The surge is real and recent: five calm months under 4,400 gave way to a February dip (3,020) and then a steep climb — September peaked at 14,934, and October is already at 13,606 with days left in the UTC month.

What counts as “published”?

Date basis: CVE publication date in UTC months. Rejected or withdrawn tombstones (18,638) are excluded from volume. Buckets never shift with the display timezone — the selector only re-renders instants, not month boundaries.

Showing windowMay 2026 – Oct 2026 · 62,521 published

Why this matters

Triage capacity does not scale with disclosure volume. When monthly output triples in eight months, “patch everything” stops being a plan — the rest of this page is how to choose what goes first.

02Severity mix · vendor-supplied only

Severity mix

Most CVEs never get a severity label: of 179,288 scored records, medium and high dominate (83,820 + 70,380), criticals are a sharp minority (16,310) — and the unscored majority below dwarfs all of them.

Whose severity is this?

Primary CNA CVSS only, by policy. Records with only ADP or NVD scores still show as unscored — strict, but honest about provenance. “None (110)” and “legacy (123)” are real edge bands, kept visible rather than rounded away.

215,998

unscored — no primary CNA metric. That is 54.6% of the corpus. Treat these as unknown severity, never as “probably low”. The EPSS forecast below is often the only numeric signal they carry.

Why this matters

Severity filters that exclude unscored records silently drop half the corpus. Any workflow built on “critical first” must say what it does with the 216k unknowns — or it is a coin flip dressed as policy.

03Exploit forecast · EPSS, model v2026.06.15

EPSS base rate

Exploitation is forecast to be rare: 377,908 of 395,249 scored CVEs (95.6%) sit under 10% probability. The long tail above 50% is just 3,420 records — small enough to read, important enough to watch.

What is EPSS?

A dated forecast (score date 2026-10-24) of exploitation in the next 30 days — a probability, not a verdict. Unknown renders as explicit null, never zero. High EPSS without KEV means “model is worried, no confirmed exploitation yet”.

377,908 under 10% · 95.6% 10–50%: 13,921 50%+: 3,420 — the watchable tail

Why this matters

A low EPSS is the normal state of a CVE, not good news about any specific one. Prioritization lives in the tail: cross the 50%+ set against KEV and new-critical lists before it crosses you.

04Confirmed exploitation · CISA KEV

KEV rhythm

Known exploitation arrives in a steady drumbeat, then spikes: 11–31 additions a month for nearly a year — then 43 in September 2026, the loudest month in this window.

What does KEV membership mean?

CISA catalogs vulnerabilities with confirmed in-the-wild exploitation. 1,756 listed = 0.44% of published CVEs. Absence from KEV is not absence of exploitation — only absence of confirmation.

Why this matters

KEV is the shortest queue on this page and the hardest to argue with. If a KEV-listed CVE touches your stack, it outranks every forecast on this page — including a 2% EPSS like the October additions below.
